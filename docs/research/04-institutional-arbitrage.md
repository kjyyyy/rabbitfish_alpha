# Institutions Buy Access; Your Lab Buys Discipline

*Not financial advice. This is an educational synthesis of published research, trade-press reporting and the output of a personal research prototype. It recommends no trade, product, broker or tax structure. Several figures are secondhand summaries of paywalled reporting on non-public documents and are labelled **[reported]**; items drawn from background knowledge with no source retrieved are labelled **[unverified]** and should be checked before anyone relies on them. UK tax and regulatory points are general information only.*

The popular model of Jane Street and Citadel Securities as spread-capture machines is about a third right, and the other two-thirds is the part a solo researcher should care about. Across the non-bank trading cohort in 2025, **market-making produced $30.2bn of revenue while proprietary trading and investment activity produced $84.3bn** ([Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/non-bank-trading-firms-surge-as-jane-street-and-citadel-securities-drive-record-114bn-revenue-pool/)). The marginal dollar at these firms now comes from taking risk with cheap capital and structural access, not from quoting — and risk-taking on medium-horizon signals is far less latency-sensitive than quoting is. That is the good news. The bad news is that every one of those structural positions is *bought*: **$2.6bn of payment for order flow over two years at one firm** ([The TRADE](https://www.thetradenews.com/citadel-securities-forks-out-2-6-billion-annually-for-payment-for-order-flow-and-most-of-its-on-options/)), **authorised-participant status gated to three to five firms per ETF** ([BIS](https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt2103d.htm)), **$29.9bn of members' equity and $10.7bn of debt behind the inventory** ([The DESK](https://www.fi-desk.com/jane-street-issues-us1-35-billion-bond-as-market-makers-bulk-up-balance-sheets/)). Alpha-lab cannot buy any of it, and after the v0.3 portfolio layer it no longer needs to pretend otherwise: the lab now does the research-dependent half — a price-only risk model, style neutralisation, no-trade bands, participation caps, square-root impact and capacity curves — and its own trial run produced the most honest result in the project. **Construction beat signal research (IR 0.18 → 0.30 from banding alone), 62% of the "alpha" turned out to be style exposure, and the market-neutral book lost money against cash.** That is the institutional lesson reproduced at 1/10,000 of institutional size, and it sets the realistic ceiling far lower than the backtest headlines suggest.

## Only a third of the $114bn comes from quoting spreads

Start with scale, because it disciplines everything downstream. Non-bank trading firms booked **$114bn of revenue in 2025, up 45% year on year**, against **$260.7bn at traditional banks, up 13%** ([Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/non-bank-trading-firms-surge-as-jane-street-and-citadel-securities-drive-record-114bn-revenue-pool/)). Jane Street alone reported **$39.6bn of net trading revenue in 2025 against $20.5bn in 2024**, and **$16.1bn of revenue with $10.3bn of net income in Q1 2026** — a roughly 64% net margin — across about **3,000 employees**, with **net trading revenue per employee of $6.9m in 2024** ([Young & Calculated](https://youngandcalculated.substack.com/p/jane-street-booked-396bn-in-2025); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-24/jane-street-snatches-wall-street-crown-with-record-39-6-billion-trading-haul)) **[reported]**. Citadel Securities booked **$12.2bn in 2025 with ~$6.5bn EBITDA on ~$21bn of trading capital**, and had **2024 net income of $4.2bn across roughly 1,800 employees** ([Young & Calculated](https://youngandcalculated.substack.com/p/jane-street-booked-396bn-in-2025); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citadel_Securities)) **[reported; none of these figures was read from a primary prospectus in the underlying research]**. The same source that reports the $114bn pool cautions that it may be **a cyclical peak rather than a structural shift** — worth holding in mind before treating any of it as a stable baseline.

Mechanically, the quoting business is volume arithmetic with a selection problem attached. The per-trade edge is a fraction of the spread; the entire enterprise is a bet that you can quote wide enough and cancel fast enough that informed flow does not eat what you collect from uninformed flow. With Citadel Securities at **over 20% of all US equity trades by end-2022** against ~$12bn of annual revenue, the implied revenue per share traded is measured in single-digit mils. That is why flow segmentation is bought rather than earned: over 2020–21 Citadel Securities paid **$877.5m for equity order flow and $1.7bn for options order flow**, roughly **a third of total industry PFOF spend**, with Susquehanna at $1.5bn and Virtu at $654m ([The TRADE](https://www.thetradenews.com/citadel-securities-forks-out-2-6-billion-annually-for-payment-for-order-flow-and-most-of-its-on-options/)). The **65/35 options-to-equities split is the tell**: the margin lives where spreads are wide and pricing is hard. The quoting mathematics itself is public and free — Avellaneda and Stoikov's inventory-skewing model has been openly available since 2008 ([Cornell](https://people.orie.cornell.edu/sfs33/LimitOrderBook.pdf)) — and it is a risk-management result about inventory, not an alpha result. It tells you how to lean your quotes; it says nothing about avoiding the person who is faster than you.

ETF arbitrage is the cleanest example of a structurally gated edge, and the bond version is not really arbitrage at all. The textbook story is BIS's: buy $10 of bonds, deliver them for one ETF share, sell at $11 ([BIS](https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt2103d.htm)). The real economics sit in the basket. For the largest bond ETF, **creation baskets average only 3% of holdings**; across corporate bond ETFs, **creation baskets cover ~20% and redemption baskets ~35%**, against near-100% for Treasury and equity funds, and **only 12% of the largest bond ETF's basket persists between consecutive creations** versus 45% for Treasury funds. APs "propose alternative bonds that facilitate their role as dealers", and **redemption baskets systematically contain shorter-duration, slightly less liquid bonds**. Meanwhile **the bid-ask spreads of bond ETFs' holdings are 17 times those of the ETF shares** (5x for equity ETFs). In March–April 2020, **tracking error on some bond ETFs exceeded 200bp against a 0.7bp historical average** ([BIS](https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt2103d.htm)), and a peer-reviewed study confirms AP regulatory and balance-sheet constraints were what limited arbitrage in that window ([Journal of Banking & Finance](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378426625001190)). So the AP is being paid for two things: a genuine NAV spread, and an option to launder illiquid inventory into a fungible liquid share. The second is invisible in the simple story and is available only to a dealer with a bond book. With **three to five active APs per ETF**, the primary market is closed to everyone else.

The best-documented recent non-latency structural edge is the Jane Street India trade, and it is documented only because SEBI alleged it was manipulation. As alleged, the firm built **~₹32,000 crore of effective short exposure through index options while holding only ~₹5,300 crore long in cash stocks and futures**, then **sold the cash position down heavily between 2:15pm and 3:30pm on expiry days at roughly 25% of market volume**, depressing the index into a cash settlement that paid off the far larger option position; SEBI's July 2025 order sought **disgorgement of over ₹4,800 crore** ([Capitalmind](https://www.capitalmind.in/insights/the-saga-of-the-jane-street-trading-scandal); [Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/our-take/7961824/sebi-unravels-jane-streets-%E2%80%98sinister%E2%80%99-trades); [Bloomberg Opinion](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/newsletters/2025-07-07/jane-street-s-indian-options-trade-was-too-good)). The context number is the point: **₹98,00,000 crore of notional index-option volume on a single day against ₹25,000 crore of premium**. Two of the three ingredients — derivative notional dwarfing cash liquidity, and cash settlement at a closing print — are visible to anybody. The third, being large enough that your own execution moves the settlement reference, is pure size. And **Jane Street's India equity-derivatives revenue exceeded $2.3bn in 2024**, more than 11% of group revenue that year ([Young & Calculated](https://youngandcalculated.substack.com/p/jane-street-booked-396bn-in-2025)) **[reported]** — evidence that these firms' returns are lumpy and concentrated in a handful of structural situations, not a smooth diversified machine. The strategy became public only because Jane Street sued Millennium over two departing traders in April 2024 and a court hearing revealed it involved India options; the case settled that December ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-04-19/jane-street-s-secret-strategy-concerns-india-hearing-reveals); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-12-05/jane-street-millennium-settle-india-options-trade-secrets-case)). Strategy IP at these firms is human-carried and surprisingly thin. The durable moat is the infrastructure and the flow.

## The dividing line: profit from knowing versus profit from being

State the rule and the whole gap analysis falls out of it. **An edge is reachable to the extent its profit comes from knowing something, and unreachable to the extent it comes from being somewhere, being large, or being licensed.** The lab's own `docs/institutional-gap.md` already uses this framing; the research behind it supports it precisely.

| Institutional edge | Gated by | Reachable? | Status in alpha-lab |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quoting, spread capture, inventory skewing | Latency, colocation, exchange membership | No | Out of scope by design |
| PFOF / internalised retail flow | Licence + ~$1bn/yr purchasing power | No | No partial version exists |
| ETF creation/redemption | AP agreement, clearing broker-dealer status | No | Premium/discount usable only as a weak on-exchange signal |
| Balance-sheet dislocation trades (March 2020 credit) | Capital, repo, dealer inventory | No | — |
| Expiry/settlement flow trading | Size sufficient to move the reference | No | — |
| Institutional borrow and locate | Prime brokerage | Mostly no | Flat 50bp assumed; bimodal reality unmodelled |
| Cross-sectional signals at daily horizon | Research | **Yes** | `expr.py`, `evaluate.py`, GP refiner, LLM proposer/critic |
| Factor/style neutralisation and risk modelling | Technique (Barra USE4 is published) | **Yes** | `risk.py` — price-only Barra substitute |
| Cost-aware construction: bands, caps, impact | Technique | **Yes** | `portfolio.py` — the largest measured gain in the project |
| Crowding monitoring | Prices only, for 3 of MSCI's 5 components | **Partly** | `crowding_r2` (Khandani–Lo turnover R²) |
| Post-trade TCA | Your own fills | **Partly** | Not built; needs live trading |
| Small capacity as a weapon | Nothing | **Yes, uniquely** | Capacity curve in v0.3 |

The published methodology makes the reachable column genuinely reachable rather than aspirational. Barra's USE4 notes give the full recipe — **cap-weighted WLS with weights proportional to √(market cap), descriptors standardised to mean 0 and standard deviation 1 using a cap-weighted mean but an equal-weighted standard deviation, Newey-West adjustment with 5 lags for volatilities and 2 for correlations, an eigenfactor adjustment scaled by a = 1.4, and EWMA half-lives of 84 days for factor volatility against 504 days for factor correlation** ([MSCI Barra USE4 Methodology Notes](https://www.top1000funds.com/wp-content/uploads/2011/09/USE4_Methodology_Notes_August_2011.pdf)). A solo builder can copy those parameters directly and substitute Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage ([scikit-learn](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.covariance.LedoitWolf.html)) for the pieces that need proprietary descriptors. Likewise the cost model: AQR's fit on **~$1.7tn of live institutional trades from 1998–2016 gives mean market impact of 9.97bps and mean implementation shortfall of 11.02bps**, with the functional form decisively concave — **a power law with exponent close to ½, log-log R² of 95%, and F-tests that easily reject the linear model** — and costs of roughly **1–2bps at 0.1% of daily volume, 9–10bps at 1%, and 20+bps at 5%** ([Frazzini, Israel & Moskowitz](https://spinup-000d1a-wp-offload-media.s3.amazonaws.com/faculty/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2021/08/Trading-Cost.pdf)). That is the anchor the lab's impact model already uses.

One caveat the report should not paper over: the impact literature is not settled. Bouchaud's result that **impact depends on total volume Q and "barely on execution schedule"** ([Bouchaud](https://bouchaud.substack.com/p/the-square-root-law-of-market-impact)) sits in real tension with Almgren-Chriss's schedule-dependent framework, and some work finds **logarithmic rather than square-root impact at large sizes** ([Scuola Normale Superiore](https://ricerca.sns.it/retrieve/9f07ef9b-0fd2-40f1-900d-a571821f9803/Beyond%20the%20square%20root.pdf)). Practitioners use Almgren-Chriss for scheduling and the square-root law for sizing and capacity. At the sizes alpha-lab operates, the disagreement is immaterial — which is itself the point.

## The trial run reproduced the pod-shop lesson at 1/10,000 scale

The v0.3 portfolio trial is the strongest evidence in this whole argument, because it is the one experiment where the lab tested the institutional machinery on its own signal rather than reading about it. Six constructions, same alpha, ¥1m book, costs including commission, spread, stamp duty and square-root impact (`/home/claude/alpha-lab/results/v0.3-2026-09-20/RESULTS.md`).

Three results matter. First, **no-trade bands cut turnover from 99% to 79% per rebalance and lifted the information ratio from 0.18 to 0.30** — a larger improvement than anything the discovery pipeline produced, obtained from a few lines of rank-band logic. It also matches the published result almost exactly: Novy-Marx and Velikov found the buy/hold spread **reduced turnover by 41% and costs by 42% on average** and called it "the single most effective cost mitigation technique" ([NBER WP 20721](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w20721/w20721.pdf)). Construction beat signal research, and the lab can now say so from its own data rather than on authority.

Second, **62% of the signal's predictive power was style exposure.** Style-neutralising cut IC from 0.062 to 0.024; the raw signal correlates **−0.50 with volatility, −0.39 with size and −0.38 with beta**. It is a low-beta, small, low-volatility bet. A pod shop would call that factor beta and decline to pay for it. Once neutralised, what remained did not survive costs — constructions 3, 4 and 6 all went negative. This is the mechanism behind the anomaly-replication literature made concrete: Hou, Xue and Zhang found that under NYSE breakpoints and value weighting, **286 of 447 anomalies (64%) are insignificant at t ≥ 1.96, and 380 (85%) fail at t ≥ 3** ([NBER WP 23394](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w23394/w23394.pdf)). Microcaps are **61% of firms but 3.28% of market capitalisation**, with cross-sectional return volatility of **19.1% against 8.9% for large stocks** — which is why a small, low-vol tilt looks like alpha until it is weighted properly.

Third, **the long/short book only looked good against a falling index.** Judged against cash — the correct benchmark for a market-neutral book — it lost 1.2%/yr with a Sharpe of −0.08. Its drawdown was much smaller (−20.1% versus −31.9% for the naive long book), so the construction demonstrably controls risk. There was simply no alpha to harvest. That is an unusually clean negative result, and it is worth more than a positive one obtained by not checking.

The capacity curve then quantifies the only structural advantage that runs the other way. Impact cost rises from **0.14%/yr at ¥100k to 0.43% at ¥1m, 1.37% at ¥10m and 4.28% at ¥100m**, where excess return turns negative; beyond ¥1bn the 5%-of-volume participation cap binds on **54–85% of trades** and the book stops being the same strategy. Capacity is roughly **¥10–100m, about £1–10m**. Because impact scales as √Q while gross alpha in percentage terms does not, cost drag grows as the square root of book size — which means **halving turnover roughly quadruples capacity**, and a book four orders of magnitude below a pod's pays essentially nothing to trade. The supporting literature makes the niche concrete rather than rhetorical: Korajczyk and Sadka put **equal-weighted momentum's break-even fund size at around $200m** against $2–3bn value-weighted and $4.5–5bn liquidity-weighted, noting equal-weighted strategies "perform the best before trading costs and the worst after" ([JF 2004](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/korajczy/htm/Korajczyk%20Sadka.jf2004.pdf)). McLean and Pontiff add that post-publication decay is **greatest in large-cap, high-dollar-volume, low-idiosyncratic-risk, dividend-paying stocks** — the ones institutions can actually trade — with returns falling **~35% after publication** across 82 characteristics from 68 studies ([McLean & Pontiff](https://www.hec.ca/finance/Fichier/McLean.pdf)).

Set against the earlier reports in this series, the picture is consistent. The LLM-mining review concluded that agents belong upstream writing factor code and never on the order button; the v0.3 ablation says the same thing from a different direction — **written hypotheses decayed least (later IC 0.021) while the GP refiner produced the most statistically strong factors and the weakest survivors (0.010)**, and **zero of 212 candidates passed Deflated Sharpe against the full trial count**. More machinery, less performance.

## Six gaps, ranked by documented damage

| Gap | Documented magnitude | Replicability for a UK solo trader |
|---|---|---|
| Point-in-time fundamentals | Annual vs daily rebalancing on a PIT "super portfolio" differs by **6.92%/yr** ([Anomaly Time](https://jacobslevycenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/Anomaly-Time.pdf)) | **Partly replicable** — SEC XBRL and as-reported vendor dimensions are inferior substitutes for Compustat Snapshot |
| Delisting returns | Omitted performance delisting returns average **−30%**; smallest size decile overstated by **5.21%/yr** ([Shumway 1997](https://www.tylergshumway.org/Shumway-DelistingBiasCRSP-1997.pdf)) | **Replicable** — and the three-way sensitivity test (0 / −30% / −100%) is nearly free |
| Live fills and TCA | Institutional live costs are **~1/10th** academic estimates; momentum break-even **$52–89bn vs $2–5bn, a 17-fold gap** ([Frazzini et al.](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~afrazzin/pdf/Trading%20Cost%20of%20Asset%20Pricing%20Anomalies%20-%20Frazzini,%20Israel%20and%20Moskowitz.pdf)) | **Partly replicable** — implementation shortfall from own fills is arithmetic; no peer-universe benchmark exists |
| Per-name borrow modelling | Borrow ranges from **0.25%/yr general collateral to 100%+ hard-to-borrow** ([QuantRocket](https://www.quantrocket.com/blog/borrow-fees-alpha/)) | **Partly replicable** — model as bimodal, exclude the likely-special decile, test untradeability |
| Intraday data | Order slicing raised optimal capacity **tenfold** in one worked retail example ([Concretum](https://concretumgroup.substack.com/p/estimating-the-capacity-of-a-trading)) | **Partly replicable** — worth little below ~5% of ADV, which is where this lab lives |
| Independent research review | **64% of anomalies fail** under conservative methodology; committees exist to enforce that ([Hou/Xue/Zhang](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w23394/w23394.pdf)) | **Not replicable** — an LLM sharing your framing is not an independent prior |

The ordering deserves defending. Delisting handling ranks above point-in-time fundamentals not because the damage is larger but because the fix is cheap and the lab's China dataset already has point-in-time membership while a US port via Yahoo would not. Borrow modelling ranks above intraday data because the signals that make a short attractive — high short interest, overvaluation, distress — are precisely the ones correlated with expensive borrow, so a naive long/short backtest systematically overstates the short leg; that correlation is the core of D'Avolio's stock-loan literature ([JFE](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X02002064)). The review gap is genuinely unbridgeable and the honest response is to substitute mechanical discipline for human adversarialism: a pre-registered hypothesis with a timestamp, an append-only trials counter feeding a Harvey-Liu Sharpe haircut ([SSRN 2345489](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2345489)), a written pre-mortem, and a pre-committed retirement rule for live signals. The lab's ledger and validity cards already do most of this; what they cannot do is disagree with you.

Two failure modes are worth defending against even though the lab is unlevered. Khandani and Lo's account of August 2007 shows a book neutral to every measured factor losing catastrophically to something no covariance matrix contained: an unleveraged 5-minute contrarian strategy lost **−18.69% on 8 August and −9.82% on 9 August**, the 60-minute version **−62.49% and −26.06%**, and an 8:1 leveraged portfolio would have lost about **31% over two days** — at a time when **4:1 to 10:1 leverage "was quite common"** ([NBER WP 14465](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w14465/w14465.pdf)). The strategies were right: the daily contrarian portfolio reversed on the 10th for a **cumulative +3.44% over the five-day period**. Only forced deleveraging turned a drawdown into a loss. The two takeaways are an argument *for* never using leverage and *against* hard stop-losses on a mean-reversion book, and they hand the lab a free crowding monitor it already implements — the **cross-sectional turnover-on-exposures regression R², which exceeded 10% in early August 2007 and at no other point that year**.

## The UK cost stack picks the strategy before the signal does

For a UK-based solo trader, the cost arithmetic is not a detail applied after strategy selection; it eliminates whole strategy classes before any signal is tested. Barber, Lee, Liu and Odean's Taiwan study is the single most decision-relevant number available: individual investors lost about **NT$935bn (US$32bn) over 1995–1999**, a **3.8 percentage point annual reduction in aggregate returns**, of which **trading losses were 27%, commissions 32%, transaction taxes 34% and market timing 7% — costs and taxes were 66% of the total** ([RFS 2009](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers%20current%20versions/justhowmuchdoindividualinvestorslose_rfs_2009.pdf)). Institutions in the same market earned **1.5 percentage points a year of abnormal return after commissions and taxes**. Two-thirds of the retail deficit was not bad stock-picking.

Translating to the UK: **stamp duty of 0.5% on cash share purchases** (AIM exempt) is already configured in `uk_ftse350.yaml`, and at 400% annual two-way turnover it alone costs roughly 100bp a year — an order of magnitude above institutional market impact, and disqualifying for anything rebalanced monthly. **[The 0.5% rate and the CFD exemption were not verified against HMRC in the underlying research — check before relying on either.]** The derivative route removes stamp duty and introduces a worse problem. One representative broker charges **"3% plus the equivalent base rate" on long CFD positions and "3% minus the rate benchmark" on shorts**, with SONIA as the GBP benchmark, plus **0.08% commission on UK shares with a £10 minimum** ([StoneX/City Index](https://www.stonex.com/en-gb/cfd-trading/costs-of-cfd-trading/)). The two spreads do not cancel — they add. A dollar-neutral CFD book therefore pays **roughly 3% of gross exposure per year in financing regardless of the level of rates**, which is a hurdle that would erase the edge measured anywhere in the v0.3 trial. The **£10 commission minimum** compounds it: a position must be at least £12,500 for the minimum not to bind at 0.08%, which caps the number of names a small book can hold, which caps breadth, which by the fundamental law caps achievable IR. This is a concrete, arithmetic reason a retail long/short equity book cannot be a scaled-down institutional one.

The wrappers are the one place where the UK gives something back. The **ISA allowance is £20,000** across all ISA types, the **CGT annual exempt amount £3,000**, the **dividend allowance £500** with the basic dividend rate rising from **8.75% to 10.75%** in 2026/27, and the **pension annual allowance £60,000** or 100% of earnings; from **April 2027 the cash ISA subscription limit falls to £12,000 for the under-65s**, pushing the remainder toward stocks-and-shares ([Bestinvest](https://www.bestinvest.co.uk/news/key-tax-allowances-for-investors-in-the-202627-tax-year); [Fidelity UK](https://www.fidelity.co.uk/markets-insights/personal-finance/personal-finance/20262027-tax-allowances-whats-changed/)). Running inside an ISA or SIPP removes CGT and dividend tax on wrapped assets entirely, which attacks the Barber decomposition's largest single component directly. **[CGT rate figures in the retrieved source were internally inconsistent and should be checked with HMRC.]** Combine the wrapper with the banding result the lab already measured, and you have addressed the two dominant documented causes of retail underperformance before writing a single new factor.

## Verdict: a low ceiling, honestly measured, with three moves that matter

The realistic ceiling is modest and the lab's own numbers already bracket it. A long-only, low-turnover, banded, large/mid-cap cross-sectional book with price-only signals, run at £100k–£1m inside an ISA, with delisting-corrected data and next-open execution, should be expected to deliver a **net-of-cost Sharpe in roughly the 0.3–0.6 range**, and anything materially above that in a backtest should be read as evidence of methodological error rather than skill. **This range is an inference from the constraint set — price-only signals concentrate in momentum and reversal, which are the high-turnover category where only two of Novy-Marx and Velikov's strategies achieve positive net excess returns, and which are the most heavily published and therefore most decayed — not a figure found in any study. No source reporting typical IC or IR for daily price-only cross-sectional equity signals as a class was located, and that is the largest unfilled gap in the underlying research.** The lab's best construction delivered IR 0.30 on a signal that turned out to be 62% style. Strip the style and the remainder did not pay for its costs. That is the honest current state, and the market-neutral variant's negative Sharpe against cash says the £1–10m capacity figure describes the *strategy's* headroom, not a forecast that the strategy earns anything within it.

Three moves have the best ratio of documented effect to effort, and they are not signal research. **First, fix the data before the model.** A delisting-corrected, survivorship-free universe with the three-way sensitivity test costs a weekend and defends against a documented 5.21%/yr overstatement in the smallest decile; porting to US data via a vendor that ships delisted tickers is the enabling step, and the roadmap already lists it. **Second, run the thirteen-week forward pre-registration to completion.** The 2025–26 holdout has been viewed, so hash-stamped weekly signals committed before Monday's open are the only clean out-of-sample evidence left; without them every claim in `results/` stays at the research-aid tier permanently, and with the GP refiner demonstrably fitting the discovery window, forward evidence is the only test that distinguishes the two. **Third, decide the market and the wrapper before the next signal round.** The China dataset is a free, survivorship-safe test bed that a UK retail trader cannot trade; the UK cash-equity route carries 0.5% stamp duty per purchase; the CFD route carries ~3% of gross per year. Choosing among them fixes the cost model that every subsequent backtest must clear, and running the ISA-wrapped, low-turnover, long-only variant first is the configuration where the documented retail advantages — near-zero impact, no forced deleveraging, no career risk, capacity no institution wants — are the only advantages you need.

## Conclusion

The most useful thing the institutional comparison yields is not a list of techniques to copy but a change in what counts as progress. These firms' returns are lumpy and concentrated — a single country's derivatives line was over 11% of Jane Street's 2024 revenue — and the concentration sits wherever access, capital or size is the mechanism. Strip those away and what remains is a research process whose components are published: Barra's estimation parameters, AQR's cost curve, Novy-Marx and Velikov's banding, Khandani and Lo's crowding statistic. Alpha-lab now runs all four. What it found by running them is that its signal was mostly style, its neutralised residual did not pay for itself, and its cheapest improvement came from trading less. Those are the same three findings an institutional risk committee would have delivered, arrived at mechanically and at a scale where being wrong costs nothing.

That reframes the remaining work. The lab's competitive position is not "a small pod shop" but "a research instrument operating in a capacity band no pod shop can enter, with costs that can be engineered to near zero and a data foundation that currently cannot support the fundamental signals where the surviving alpha mostly lives." The binding constraint is data quality and out-of-sample evidence, not modelling sophistication — which is fortunate, because data quality is purchasable at retail prices and forward evidence only requires patience. The trap to avoid is the one the v0.3 ablation already caught: every additional layer of machinery so far has made results worse, and the discipline to keep the gates harder than the miner is the only part of the institutional stack that transfers in full.
